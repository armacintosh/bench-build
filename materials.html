1. Project Overview & Interactive 3D CAD Studio

Module 01 // Studio

This piece is an overbuilt, multi-functional 80.00" Long × 29.00" Deep basement rec-room and playroom platform that converts in 10 seconds between a deep lounging sofa, a 1,000+ lb capacity jump-proof bench, and a flat play/coffee table—while storing up to five large soft baskets underneath with zero center front post in the way.

Architecture // 01

Sunken 1.00" Cushion Tray

A 3/4" Birch Plywood deck sits directly atop the coplanar 2x4 torsion frame (Z = 19.00"..19.75"), while a stain-grade 1x6 outer trim apron rises to Z = 20.75". This creates a 1.00" perimeter tray that locks the 4" foam seat cushion in place without Velcro.

Architecture // 02

“Wheelbarrow” Hidden Mobility

The 2 front legs sit on high-friction 3/8" solid rubber grip pads (100% locked when kids jump). The 3 rear legs house recessed fixed-axle rollers protruding exactly 3/8". Lift the front apron by 1 inch to roll the entire couch effortlessly on the rear wheels!

Architecture // 03

Drop-In 10° Slanted Backrests

Two independent 40"-wide backrest modules use doubled 2x6 uprights (3.00" wide × 5.50" front-to-back). Each upright has a 1-7/16" bottom peg that drops into the 1.50" slot, a 90° solid wood rear shoulder ledge that sits flat on top of the couch, and a 10° front lean where people lean back.

6. Step-by-Step Assembly Roadmap (Zero-Context DIY Walkthrough)

Module 04 // Roadmap

Follow these 7 sequential phases in order. Every phase includes Why We Do This, Exact Fasteners to Use, Pro-Tips / Common Mistakes to Avoid, a dedicated step diagram, and a one-click button to isolate that phase in the Interactive 3D CAD Studio.

PHASE 01 // 07 Prepping the 5 Legs: 18-5/8" Cuts, Roller Mortises, Grip Pads & Half-Lap Notches

💡 Why We Do This:
Cutting the 1.50" × 3.50" half-lap notch into the top of all five 4x4 posts creates a solid wood-on-wood shoulder at Z = 15.50". When kids jump on the couch, the vertical load transfers directly into the end-grain of the 4x4 posts rather than relying on screw shear!
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 3× Rear Recessed Rollers (installed into bottom of Leg_BL, Leg_BC, Leg_BR with included mounting screws).
• 2× Front Rubber Grip Pads (3.5"×3.5"×3/8") attached to bottom of Leg_FL & Leg_FR with contact cement + recessed 1" pan-head screws.
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
Clamp all 5 cut legs (18.625" / 18-5/8") side-by-side flush at the top, set your circular saw blade depth to exactly 1-1/2" (1.50"), and gang-cut the 6–8 kerf passes across all 5 legs at once! Every notch will be 100% identical in 5 minutes.
  1. Crosscut the 5 Legs [Part A]: From your single 8-ft 4x4 post, measure and cut five pieces to 18.625" (18-5/8"). Label two as Leg_FL / Leg_FR (Front) and three as Leg_BL / Leg_BC / Leg_BR (Rear).
  2. Bore the 3 Rear Roller Pockets: On the bottom end-grain of the 3 Rear Legs, trace your recessed roller housing centered left-to-right and aligned so the roller axle rolls front-to-back (along Y). Use a Forstner bit or plunge router to mortise the pocket to the exact depth where the wheel protrudes 0.375" (3/8") below the wood. Screw the 3 rollers in place.
  3. Attach the 2 Front Grip Pads: Fasten the 3.50" × 3.50" × 3/8" solid rubber pads to the bottom of the 2 Front Legs. Verify that all 5 legs now stand exactly 19.000" tall (18.625" wood + 0.375" foot).
  4. Cut the 1.50" × 3.50" Half-Lap Notches: Measure 3.50" down from the top of each leg. Set your circular saw blade depth to 1.50", make kerf cuts every 1/2" across the top 3.50" zone, knock out the wood wafers with a hammer, and pare the face smooth with a sharp chisel. Test-fit a scrap 2x4—it should sit dead-flush with both the top and face of the post.
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 01 All 5 legs measure 19.000" total height w/ 1.50"×3.50" top half-lap shoulders

PHASE 02 // 07 Laminating the Unbreakable Front Beam & Assembling Front/Rear Portals

💡 Why We Do This:
To achieve a massive 71.50" unobstructed front basket opening with zero center front leg, we glue and screw the 71.50" Inner Front Beam [Part C] directly behind the 78.50" Outer Front Rail [Part B], creating a monolithic 3.00" × 3.50" structural header.
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 10× #10 × 3.00" GRK R4 Structural Screws (2 per leg notch, driven from inside of post into rail, or through rail into post + glue).
• 20× #10 × 2.75" (2-3/4") GRK Structural Screws + Titebond glue in a staggered W-pattern every 8" for beam lamination.
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
If either front 2x4 has a slight crown (curve along its edge), orient both crowns pointing UP (+Z) so any downward seating weight flattens the beam rather than sagging!
  1. Build the Front Portal: Lay Leg_FL and Leg_FR flat on your workbench/floor with their notches facing up. Spread wood glue in both notches, seat the 78.50" Outer Front Rail [Part B] flush into the notches (X = 0.75"..79.25"), check for 90° squareness with a speed square, and drive two 3.00" structural screws per joint.
  2. Laminate the Inner Front Beam [Part C]: Spread a generous coat of wood glue along the back face of the Outer Front Rail between the two front legs (X = 4.25"..75.75"). Drop the 71.50" Inner Front Beam [Part C] tightly between the inner faces of Leg_FL and Leg_FR, clamp flush at the top edge (Z = 19.00"), and drive twenty 2.75" structural screws from the inside face in a staggered W-pattern every 8.0".
  3. Build the 3-Leg Rear Portal: Lay Leg_BL (X = 0.75..4.25"), the 5th Center-Back Leg Leg_BC (X = 38.25..41.75", centered at X = 40.00"), and Leg_BR (X = 75.75..79.25") with their rear-facing notches up. Glue and fasten the 78.50" Main Structural Back Rail [Part B] into all three notches using two 3.00" structural screws per leg.
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 02 Front 2-Leg Portal & Rear 3-Leg Portal square at 90° with 71.50" clear front span

PHASE 03 // 07 Building the Coplanar Ladder Joist Grid & Rear Drop-In Mortise Sleeve

💡 Why We Do This:
Because all 2x4 rails, joists, and spacer blocks share the exact same elevation (Z = 15.50" to 19.00"), you never have to rip a single 2x4! And using a scrap block as a temporary spacer gauge while clamping the rear pocket channel guarantees your 4 backrest slots will never bind.
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 24× 2.50" (2-1/2") Heavy-Duty Pocket Screws (2 per end on the 2 Side Rails [Part D] and 5 Cross-Joists [Part E]).
• 14× #10 × 3.00" Structural Screws to lock the 5 Spacer Blocks [Part F] and Rear Pocket Backer Rail [Part B].
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
NEVER rely on a tape measure alone when spacing the 4 rear backrest slots! Use two scrap pieces of 2x6 or 2x4 screwed together (`3.00"` wide) plus a 1/8" cardboard shim (`3-1/8"` total width × `1.50"` front-to-back) and clamp that block inside each slot while screwing the adjacent spacer block. When you pull the gauge block out, every slot is guaranteed to be `3-1/8" × 1-1/2"`!
  1. Connect Front & Rear Portals with Side Rails [Part D]: Stand the Front and Rear Portals upright on a flat floor. Glue and pocket-screw the two 19.50" Side Frame Rails [Part D] between the front and rear corner legs (Y = 4.25"..23.75"), keeping the top faces 100% coplanar at Z = 19.00".
  2. Install the 5 Coplanar Cross-Joists [Part E]: Mark joist centerlines at X = 16.00", 28.00", 40.00", 52.00", and 64.00" along the Inner Front Beam (Y = 3.75") and Main Back Rail (Y = 23.75"). Glue and pocket-screw the five 20.00" Cross-Joists [Part E] flush with the top of the frame (Z = 19.00").
  3. Assemble the Rear Mortise Pocket Channel (Y = 25.25"..28.25"): Behind the Main Back Rail, glue and screw the 5 Spacer Blocks [Part F] at their exact X-positions to leave four 3-1/8" (3.125") L-R × 1-1/2" (1.50") F-B slots:
    • Spacer 1 (5.50") at X = 0.75..6.25" → leaves Slot 1 at X = 6.25..9.375"
    • Spacer 2 (21.25") at X = 9.375..30.625" → leaves Slot 2 at X = 30.625..33.75"
    • Spacer 3 (12.50" Center) at X = 33.75..46.25" → leaves Slot 3 at X = 46.25..49.375"
    • Spacer 4 (21.25") at X = 49.375..70.625" → leaves Slot 4 at X = 70.625..73.75"
    • Spacer 5 (5.50") at X = 73.75..79.25"
  4. Close the Mortise Sleeve: Glue and screw the 78.50" Rear Pocket Backer Rail [Part B] (Y = 26.75"..28.25") across the back of all 5 spacer blocks using 3.00" structural screws, checking each of the 4 slots (`3-1/8" × 1-1/2"`) with your gauge block as you go.
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 03 All joists coplanar at Z = 19.00"; 3.00"-wide doubled 2x6 gauge slides smoothly into all 4 rear slots

PHASE 04 // 07 Installing the 3/4" Birch Plywood Torsion-Box Seat Deck

💡 Why We Do This:
Gluing and screwing the 78.50" × 23.75" × 3/4" Birch Plywood sheet [Part G] across the coplanar 2x4 grid locks the top flange and bottom framing into a rigid stressed-skin torsion box, multiplying stiffness across the 71.50" span.
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 45× #8 × 1.75" (1-3/4") Countersunk Wood Screws spaced every 8.0" along perimeter rails and all 5 interior joists + continuous bead of Titebond wood glue.
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
Notice that the plywood deck is 23.75" deep (spanning Y = 0.75" to Y = 24.50", halfway across the Main Back Rail). Do NOT cover Y = 25.25"..26.75" with the seat deck—that rear zone stays open for the 4 drop-in backrest slots!
  1. Dry-Fit & Square the Frame: Lay the 78.50" × 23.75" Birch Plywood Seat Deck [Part G] on top of the 2x4 frame (Z = 19.00"..19.75"). Align its front and side edges flush with the outside of the 2x4 perimeter (X = 0.75..79.25", Y = 0.75..24.50")—the factory-square plywood sheet automatically squares the entire bench!
  2. Snap Chalk/Pencil Lines for Joists: Mark the 5 interior joist centerlines (X = 16", 28", 40", 52", 64") across the top face of the plywood.
  3. Glue & Screw Down: Lift the deck, run a 1/4" bead of wood glue along the top of all perimeter rails and joists, set the deck back down, and drive #8 × 1-3/4" screws every 8.0" around the perimeter and along each joist line. Countersink every screw head flush.
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 04 78.50"×23.75" Birch deck glued & screwed at Z = 19.75" with rear mortise channel unobstructed

PHASE 05 // 07 Wrapping the Blind-Fastened 1x6 Stain-Grade Apron & Rear Pocket Cap Trim

💡 Why We Do This:
Mounting the 5.50" tall 1x6 apron from Z = 15.25" to Z = 20.75" hides the bottom of the rough 2x4 frame by 1/4" while projecting exactly 1.00" above the plywood deck (Z = 19.75"..20.75") to form the sunken cushion tray—with zero visible screws on the outside!
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 36× #8 × 1.875" (1-7/8") Screws driven strictly from the inside of the 2x4 rails into the back of the 1x6 apron.
• 1-1/4" Finish Brads + Glue (or countersunk 1-1/2" trim screws) for the 1x2 Rear Pocket Cap Strips [Parts J & M].
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
To get a laser-consistent 1.00" cushion lip all the way around without measuring each clamp, place four 1.00" thick scrap blocks on top of the plywood deck near the edges and clamp your 1x6 apron flush with the top of those blocks! (Note: On the Rear Apron [Part H], either mount its top flush at Z = 19.75" behind the 4 slots or notch four 3-1/8" × 1.00" reliefs so the 90° solid wood rear shoulder ledge of each 2x6 upright rests flat on Z = 19.75".)
  1. Cap the Rear Mortise Channel [Parts J & M]: Glue and brad-nail the 78.50" 1x2 Rear Pocket Cap Strip [Part J] atop the Rear Pocket Backer Rail (Y = 26.75"..28.25", Z = 19.00"..19.75") and the five 1x2 Spacer Caps [Part M] (5.50", 21.25", 12.50", 21.25", 5.50") directly atop the 5 spacer blocks. Now the entire rear deck surface around the 4 open mortise slots sits coplanar with the Z = 19.75" Birch Plywood deck!
  2. Miter & Clamp the Front & Side 1x6 Aprons [Parts H & I]: Cut the two 80.00" Front/Rear Aprons [Part H] and two 29.00" Side Aprons [Part I] with crisp 45° mitered ends (long-point to long-point). Clamp the Front & Side Aprons in place so their top edges sit at Z = 20.75" (1.00" above the plywood deck) and their bottom edges sit at Z = 15.25" (0.25" below the 2x4 rail).
  3. Drive the Blind Inside Screws: Crawl/reach underneath the frame and drive #8 × 1-7/8" screws through the 1.50" thick 2x4 perimeter rails into the back of the 0.75" 1x6 apron every 10"–12". Glue the 45° miter corners tightly with wood glue and clamp until cured.

PHASE 06 // 07 Cutting & Laminating the Doubled 2x6 Uprights & Two 40" Drop-In Backrests

💡 Why We Do This:
Each 2x6 stands with its 5.50" width running Front-to-Back and has a 90° solid wood rear shoulder ledge that rests flat on top of the couch (`Z = 19.75"`), while the front side leans back 10.0°. Gluing two 1.50" halves face-to-face creates a 3.00"-wide post with `6.75 sq in` of solid wood resting on the couch surface!
🔩 Exact Fasteners & Hardware:
• 20× #10 × 2.75" (2-3/4") Screws + Wood Glue (5 per upright to laminate the 8 cut halves into 4 doubled 3.00"-wide posts).
• 16× #8 × 1.75" (1-3/4") Countersunk Wood Screws (4 per upright, driven through the 3/4" Birch panel into the 3.00"-wide 10° front face of each post) + wood glue.
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
Cut thefirst `2x6` half carefully and use it as a tracing template for the other 7 halves! And when attaching the Birch plywood panels, drop the laminated uprights into the couch slots first so the couch itself holds them at the exact `24.375"` center-to-center spacing.
  1. Cut 8 Identical Halves from Your Two 2x6 Boards [Part K]: Follow SVG 2 in Section 4 to crosscut both 8-ft 2x6 boards into eight 23.50" rectangles (`5.50"` wide). On each piece, make only 2 saw cuts:
    • Cut #1 (Back Side — 90° Solid Wood Shoulder Ledge): Measure up 4.25" from the bottom along the back edge, and cut a 90° L-notch that leaves a 1-7/16" (1.44") front-to-back peg at the bottom and a 4-1/16" (4.06") flat horizontal solid wood shoulder ledge at Z = 4.25". Leave the upper 19.25" back edge uncut (`90°` vertical)!
    • Cut #2 (Front Side — 10.0° Recline Angle): Starting at the front edge right at 4.25" from the bottom (the top of the peg), draw a straight line to a point 3-3/8" (3.39") back from the front corner at the top edge (leaving `2-1/8" / 2.11"` of wood at the top) and rip that straight 10.0° taper.
  2. Laminate in Pairs to Make Four 3.00"-Wide Uprights: Pair the 8 identical halves into 4 sets of 2. Spread wood glue between each pair, clamp their bottom pegs (`3.00" × 1.44"`) and `90°` rear shoulder ledges flush, and drive five 2-3/4" screws through each pair. Add a 1/8" chamfer around the bottom tip of each peg and test-fit in Slots 1–4—the `90°` solid wood rear shoulder ledge (`2.25" × 3.00" = 6.75 sq in`) will sit rock-solid on the couch top (`Z = 19.75"`)!
  3. Attach the Two 39.25" × 15.00" Birch Panels [Part L]: Insert the Left pair of uprights into Slots 1 & 2 and the Right pair into Slots 3 & 4. Place a temporary 3.50" tall scrap 2x4 block flat on the plywood seat deck so the bottom edge of each 39.25" × 15.00" Birch Plywood panel starts at Z = 23.25" (allowing the 4.00" foam seat cushion to tuck cleanly underneath!). Glue, clamp, and screw each panel into the wide `3.00"` front face of its two uprights with four #8 × 1-3/4" screws per upright.
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 06 4 doubled 3.00" uprights rest flat on their 90° solid wood rear ledges & recline 10° on the front

PHASE 07 // 07 Sanding, Staining/Finishing, and Waxing the Backrest Tenons

💡 Why We Do This:
Rounding over the top inside edge of the 1x6 cushion lip prevents fabric wear on your cushion cover, while rubbing dry paraffin wax onto the bare wood tenons gives a silky, non-stick glide that never gums up like oil or grease.
🔩 Exact Consumables:
• 80 → 120 → 180 Grit Sandpaper Discs
• Pre-Stain Conditioner + Wood Stain + 3 Coats Water-Based Matte/Satin Polyurethane
• Household Paraffin Wax Block
🛠️ Pro-Tip / Mistake to Avoid:
Wrap painter's tape around the bottom 4.25" tenons of the four backrest uprights before applying stain or polyurethane! Thick film finishes inside mortise-and-tenon joints cause sticky wood-on-wood friction in humid summer weather.
  1. Orbital Sanding Progression: Sand all visible 1x6 aprons, 1x2 cap strips, and Birch backrest panels starting with 80-grit (leveling miter joints), then 120-grit, and finishing with 180-grit. Soften all sharp top edges to a kid-friendly 1/8" radius.
  2. Stain & Clear Topcoat: Vacuum all dust, wipe with a damp tack cloth, apply pre-stain wood conditioner (if using pine/poplar), stain to your desired tone, and seal with 3 coats of water-based polyurethane (sanding lightly with 220-grit between coats).
  3. Wax the Tenons & Drop In the Cushion: Peel the painter's tape off the four 4.25" backrest tenons and rub a block of dry paraffin wax firmly over all four faces of each tenon. Drop the two 40" backrests into the 4 slots, drop your 78.0" × 23.5" × 4.0" foam cushion into the 1.00" recessed tray, and test your 1-inch wheelbarrow lift!
✓ DOD CHECKPOINT 07 Waxed tenons, 4" cushion seated in 1.00" tray, 1-inch wheelbarrow lift verified
